# Setting up CopyBlip on iPhone

Once set up, opening CopyBlip is all it takes:

- **You copied something on the iPhone:** CopyBlip sends it to your computers.
- **Otherwise:** CopyBlip gets whatever you last copied on a computer and puts it on the iPhone clipboard.

Then paste as usual. The app shows this guide the first time it opens, and again from **Setup Guide** in Settings (the gear button).

## 1. Allow pasting without asking

iOS normally asks "Allow Paste?" each time an app reads something copied in another app. Allowing it once in Settings stops the prompt.

1. Open **Settings › Apps › CopyBlip**, or tap **Open CopyBlip Settings** in the guide.
2. Tap **Paste from Other Apps**.
3. Choose **Allow**.

If **Paste from Other Apps** isn't listed, iOS hasn't asked CopyBlip about pasting yet. Copy some text, open CopyBlip, tap **Allow Paste** once, and the setting will appear.

If you tap **Don't Allow** on the prompt, CopyBlip won't send that copy and will open this guide. It doesn't ask again for the same copy.

## 2. Allow Local Network

CopyBlip finds your computers on your Wi-Fi. The first time it looks, iOS asks permission. Choose **Allow**.

If you chose **Don't Allow**, turn on **Local Network** under **Settings › Apps › CopyBlip**.

## 3. Use it

- **iPhone to computer:** copy, open CopyBlip, then paste on the computer.
- **Computer to iPhone:** copy on the computer, open CopyBlip, then paste on the iPhone.

The iPhone and computer need to be on the same Wi-Fi, with CopyBlip running on the computer.

If something didn't sync, the main screen has buttons to send or get manually. The send button is iOS's own paste button, so it never shows the paste prompt.

## Good to know

- **Handoff:** if your Mac uses the same Apple account as your iPhone, Universal Clipboard may move copies between them before CopyBlip does. That's harmless. When the iPhone sends back what the Mac already has, the Mac doesn't rewrite its clipboard.
- **Conflicts:** if you copied on both devices since last opening CopyBlip, the iPhone's copy wins.
